# Week 10 Presenter Notes: Workflow Audit and Measured Improvement

## Opening
“Improve a personal AI engineering workflow using evidence instead of adopting a tool or rule because it sounds sophisticated.” Ask students to name the evidence they bring from the previous week. If that evidence is missing, identify the recovery task before introducing more scope.

## Explain the Three Decisions
### Audit the record
Identify recurring problems in context, requirements, tests, tool use, coordination, or review. Distinguish correlation from a demonstrated cause. Revisit the initial Week 1 method and the Week 3/4 supervision choices.

Ask: “What would convince you this decision is correct for your project?” Follow with: “What would make you change your mind?” Use the student's actual domain rather than an abstract ideal architecture.

### Run a fair comparison
Choose one bounded task and change one workflow factor. Use the same baseline and acceptance checks; record time, review effort, tool usage where observable, defects, and caveats. A single trial supports a local decision, not a universal ranking.

Ask: “What would convince you this decision is correct for your project?” Follow with: “What would make you change your mind?” Use the student's actual domain rather than an abstract ideal architecture.

### Write operational rules
Turn findings into actionable steps with triggers, inputs, checks, and escalation. Keep skills and project instructions concise; retire ineffective rules. Explain when to work in the loop, on the loop, or with multiple agents.

Ask: “What would convince you this decision is correct for your project?” Follow with: “What would make you change your mind?” Use the student's actual domain rather than an abstract ideal architecture.

## During the Demonstration
Pause before the decisive check. Ask for a prediction, run it, and compare the result. Narrate why you let the agent continue or why you intervene; avoid narrating every keystroke. Label prepared defects and recordings honestly.

## Address the Misconception
Do not convert “this run was faster” into “this tool is always better.” Ask what was held constant and what could explain the result.

## Practice Debrief
Ask each pair for one supported claim, one unresolved risk, and one next action. Make the feedback specific to the submitted evidence and the current milestone. Do not demand later-week skills prematurely.

## Closing
“Week 11 tests whether students can explain and adapt this workflow under interview constraints.” Open `homework.md` and point out the core criteria, 5–10 hour budget, and submission artifacts. Record setup blockers and evidence gaps for focused follow-up.
